# Thermal Runaway in Lithium-Ion Batteries

## Definition
Thermal runaway is a catastrophic failure mode in lithium-ion batteries characterized by an uncontrollable, self-accelerating chain of exothermic chemical reactions. Once initiated, the battery's internal temperature rises extremely rapidly, leading to outgassing, structural failure, fire, or explosion.

## Stages of Thermal Runaway
1. **Initiation (Abuse):** The process begins due to thermal abuse (overheating), electrical abuse (overcharging or rapid over-discharging), or mechanical abuse (puncture/crush). 
2. **SEI Layer Breakdown (ca. 80-120°C):** The Solid Electrolyte Interphase (SEI) layer on the anode begins to decompose. This reaction is mildly exothermic and raises the internal temperature further.
3. **Separator Melting (ca. 130-150°C):** The polymer separator between the anode and cathode melts, causing widespread internal short circuits. This triggers massive Joule heating.
4. **Electrolyte Breakdown and Cathode Decomposition (ca. 150-200°C+):** The electrolyte breaks down into flammable gases. The metal oxide cathode begins to release oxygen, feeding combustion without the need for atmospheric air.
5. **Runaway:** The reactions become self-sustaining and violent. Temperatures can spike beyond 600°C within seconds.

## Prevention
Preventing thermal runaway requires strict adherence to operating limits. A robust BTMS utilizes predictive models to foresee temperature spikes and preemptively engages emergency cooling measures before the critical 60°C threshold is reached.
