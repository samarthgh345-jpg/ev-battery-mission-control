# Battery Cooling Strategies

## Active vs. Passive Cooling
Battery cooling can be broadly categorized into active and passive systems. Passive systems rely on ambient air or phase-change materials (PCMs) to absorb heat without requiring external energy to pump a fluid. Active systems use pumps or fans to circulate a cooling medium.

## Liquid Cooling
Liquid cooling is the industry standard for modern, high-performance electric vehicles. Liquids possess a significantly higher specific heat capacity and thermal conductivity than air, making them far more effective at extracting heat from densely packed battery cells.

### Indirect Liquid Cooling
Most EVs use indirect liquid cooling, where a water-glycol mixture is circulated through cooling plates or microchannels situated between or beneath the battery cells. The coolant flow rate and coolant inlet temperature are the primary control variables for the BTMS.

### Coolant Flow Rate and Heat Transfer
Increasing the coolant flow rate (measured in kg/s) increases the Reynolds number, pushing the flow into a turbulent regime. Turbulent flow disrupts the thermal boundary layer, significantly increasing the heat transfer coefficient (W/m²K). However, higher flow rates also increase the coolant pressure drop, requiring more pumping power (energy cost). 

### Nanoparticle Coolants
Advanced experimental cooling systems may introduce nanoparticles (like Al2O3) into the coolant base fluid, creating a nanofluid. Nanofluids offer enhanced thermal conductivity, improving heat extraction efficiency at the cost of higher fluid viscosity and potential microchannel clogging.
